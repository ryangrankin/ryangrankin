
# Hi, I'm Ryan! 👋

I'm a 4th year student at the **University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa**, double majoring in **Information & Computer Sciences** and **American Studies**.

I love learning new things, figuring out how things work, and building projects that bring different ideas together. My two majors might seem pretty different, but that's actually what I enjoy most about them. Computer Science gives me the tools to build technology, while American Studies encourages me to ask questions about who that technology serves, how it shapes our lives, and why those things matter.

I'm especially interested in the space where **technology, culture, and people** intersect.

## 💻 What I've Been Building

- **Korea-Go:** A Korean language learning app designed around practical travel scenarios, cultural learning, and interactive lessons.
- **Hip-Hop Archive:** An interactive exploration of hip-hop history, artist connections, and the cultural influence of the genre.
- **Vermont Biodiversity:** An educational platform developed in collaboration with students to encourage engagement with local wildlife and the environment.
- **Shift Scheduler:** A restaurant scheduling application inspired by my own experience working in hospitality.
- **PathFinder:** A C++ project exploring interactive grids, algorithms, and visualization.

I enjoy the entire process of bringing an idea to life, from figuring out what people actually need to troubleshooting the little things that inevitably go wrong.

## 📚 Beyond the Code

My American Studies coursework has been just as important to me as my programming classes.

I'm currently researching **algorithmic bias in AI-driven hiring**, looking at how automated decision-making systems can reinforce existing inequalities and affect people's opportunities.

I'm also interested in digital culture, music, education, and the ways technology influences how we communicate and understand one another. My Hip-Hop Archive project is one example of how I like combining these interests with software development.

I don't think technology exists in a vacuum, and I want to be the kind of developer who considers both what we *can* build and what we *should* build.
